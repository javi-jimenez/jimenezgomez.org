## Introduction

The figure of the Undercover - L'Encobert in Valencian - is one of those myths that condense history, prophecy and symbolism. Emerging around the Germanías of the 16th century, his presence in the popular Valencian imagination persists as an archetype: a humble young man who embodies a radical hope for change in times of catastrophe.

## Historical origin: the Germanías

The myth was born in 1522, in the context of the Germanías Rebellion. A mysterious character, presented as a hidden heir of royal lineage, appears as a messianic leader and promise of social justice. After his violent death, the figure became a symbol and legend, reappearing in later stories and predictions.

## Prophetic tradition and Mediterranean echoes

The prophetic currents of the area—texts attributed to medieval authors, popular sermons, and local manuscripts—fueled the vision of the Undercover as a mystical 'charge' that manifests itself when the land and the community require it. In modern esoteric versions, the character is described as an ascetic: dark clothing, bare feet, medium-long hair and eyes that denote a different vision of the world.

## Contemporary connections: Parravicini and the archetype

Psychographies such as those of Benjamín Solari Parravicini and other modern visions offer parallels: the "gray man" or young visionaries appear as heralds of a spiritual transformation. Features such as green eyes become metaphors for inner vision, and black attire is interpreted in an alchemical key (nigredo), not necessarily as a sign of evil.

## Symbolism: barefoot, black, green eyes

- Black: stage of "death" of the ego and preparation for transmutation.
- Barefoot: direct contact with the earth, humility and territorial purification.
- Green eyes: association with the "emerald light" of occult knowledge or non-ordinary vision.

These combined images create the Undercover as the mystical Puer Aeternus: a wandering youth who appears to correct the course of history when structures fail.

## Valencia as a symbolic epicenter

Local stories link Valencia with spiritual catalysts (Holy Chalice, liturgical chants such as the Song of the Sibyl) and places of testing. In esoteric circles it is stated that any public manifestation of the Undercover would have connections with the Cathedral and the historic streets of the city.

## Readings and sources to delve deeper

- Parravicini's psychographies and drawings (symbolic comparisons).
- Studies on Joaquín de Fiore, Arnau de Vilanova and the apocalyptic imaginary in the Mediterranean.

## Final reflection

The Undercover functions as a liminal figure: a mixture of hidden king, ascetic and prophetic savior. More than a singular person, he represents a collective expectation of radical renewal that reappears in different moments of crisis. His description (young man in black, barefoot, green eyes, medium hair) is today a powerful image that collects historical traces and symbolic layers that cross the political, the religious and the mystical.
